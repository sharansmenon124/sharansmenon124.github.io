Elastomeric
lattice testing.

What happens when a printed honeycomb is compressed slowly, and what changes when it is struck at speed?

Undergraduate researcher · Composites Research Laboratory · Since September 2025

Building the specimens

With support from an AEROS/ASPIRE Scholarship, I designed and fabricated 144 honeycomb lattice structures using fused deposition modeling and an elastomeric plastic. The research focused on how the printed structures deform, fail, and absorb energy.

Two loading conditions

I used a material testing system for quasi-static compression tests to characterize the stress–strain response and observe how the specimens failed. For dynamic tests, I used a customized Cadex impactor to study energy absorption under high-velocity compression.

Controlling buckling

Buckling during a test can complicate interpretation of the specimen’s response. I designed and CNC-machined a fixture to control buckling so the compression measurements would better characterize the lattice material.

Conference presentation

I compiled the experimental work and findings into a research presentation for the AIAA YPSE Conference.
