Elastomeric honeycomb lattices

Composites Research Laboratory · University of Maryland

I began working on elastomeric honeycombs in September 2025 with support from an AEROS/ASPIRE Scholarship. I wanted to see how a printed lattice deforms under a load and how much energy it can absorb. I designed and printed 144 specimens using fused deposition modeling (FDM), building the cell walls layer by layer from an elastomeric plastic.

For the quasi-static tests, I compressed the specimens slowly in a material testing system. The stress–strain response records how much stress it takes to keep compressing a sample as its height changes. I used those measurements alongside observations of how the specimens failed.

One practical issue was buckling. If a specimen buckles during compression, the measured response depends in part on that motion. I designed a fixture to control buckling and CNC-machined it for the tests.

For the dynamic tests, I used a customized Cadex impactor to load the lattices at high speed. That part of the study focused on energy absorption under impact. I brought the compression and impact work together in my research presentation for the AIAA YPSE Conference.
